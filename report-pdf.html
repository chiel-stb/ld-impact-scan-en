ABOUT THIS REPORT

About this report

The L&D Impact Scan gives you insight into how Learning and Development is currently organised within your organisation — in relation to its strategic importance. It serves as an orientation tool: where can L&D currently make the greatest impact?

This report is not intended to evaluate your L&D approach or measure its "maturity level". The goal is to make visible where the gap between ambition and current practice is greatest. That is precisely where the potential lies.

The report is based entirely on the answers you provided in the scan. These have been translated into:

  • Insight into your context and priorities
  • An overview of the most relevant L&D use cases
  • Focus areas where targeted attention can deliver the greatest impact for employees and the organisation

Use this report as a basis for conversation. As a compass for priorities. And as a foundation for deciding together: where should we invest time, energy and resources most effectively?

Key terms

  • L&D Impact Model — Framework for structuring and strategically aligning your L&D approach
  • Use Case — A specific area of application within the L&D context
  • Focus use case — A use case with a comparatively large gap, where targeted attention creates the greatest value
  • Gap — The difference between strategic importance and current approach
  • Success Factors — Building blocks required to effectively set up a use case

L&D IMPACT MODEL

L&D Impact Model

The L&D Impact Model is a framework that brings structure to your L&D approach. It encompasses eight clearly defined use cases that together cover the full breadth of Learning and Development within an organisation.

In many organisations, L&D consists of individual initiatives, urgent requests and ad-hoc solutions. The L&D Impact Model provides a shared language and a fixed frame of reference for bundling these initiatives and viewing them in context. The model is not a step-by-step plan or a maturity model — there is no fixed sequence and no defined end goal.

How to read this model

For each use case, two values were determined: the strategic importance of the topic for your organisation and the current approach. The difference between the two forms the gap.

The highlighted use cases (#1, #2, #3) show where this gap is greatest. These are your focus use cases — not because something is "poorly" organised there, but because this is where, relative to your priorities, the greatest potential for impact lies.
